How good are these forecasts?

A forecast is only useful if you know how far to trust it. Here is what we can say today, kept in three separate places so they are never mixed up: live checks, replays of past events, and research tests.

Live forecasts

Live verification starts once about 30 days of forecast targets have matured. A day's forecast can only be scored after that day has passed and its demand data has been published, so the first scores need about a month of daily runs.

When it starts, this section will show, for each day ahead: the fair CRPS skill score against climatology, how reliable the top-10% chances are, and counts of caught, missed and false-alarm events over the last 30 and 90 days.

Replays: forecasts against what happened

We re-ran the system as if it were the morning of past events, using only data available at the time, then compared it with the demand that followed. These are hindcasts. A handful of events is a small sample, so read them as examples, not a verdict.

Scoring the replays…

Did the actual value land inside the nine-in-ten range?

Share of area-days where the actual value fell between the forecast's 5th and 95th percentiles, all replays pooled. A well-calibrated forecast scores about 90%. Lower means the ranges were too narrow; higher means too wide.

Unusually high days: caught, missed and false alarms

An unusually high day is one where actual peak demand went above the area's top-10% level. We count it as caught when the forecast gave it a 50% chance or more. Days 1 to 14 for every area in each replay.

Research evaluation, not live

Research skill from cross-validation and holdout tests

During development the model was tested on years it had not seen: cross-validation over 2006–2021 and a holdout over 2021–2025. Skill is the continuous ranked probability skill score (CRPSS) against climatology: 0 means no better than the long-term average for the date, 1 would be perfect.
