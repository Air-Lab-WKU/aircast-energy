Three things to know before you read the map

  1. Each shape is a grid area, not a power plant. It is the approximate area served by a balancing authority, the organisation that keeps supply and demand matched there. Smaller areas sit on top of larger ones they overlap, and the smallest also get a dot. Northern Nevada (Sierra Pacific) has no outline and is shown as a dot; grey areas are run by authorities this site does not forecast.
  2. Colour is a chance, not a promise. An area at 30% means that, out of ten forecasts like this one, about three would see demand land in that area's top 10% for the time of year. On an ordinary day the chance is 10%.
  3. This is a research system. It is new, the mode it runs in day to day has not yet been tested in live use, and it should not drive dispatch decisions. Use the grid operators' own forecasts for that.

On an ordinary day the chance of landing in the top 10% is 10%, in the top 5% it is 5%, and in the top 1% it is 1%. Each area has its own levels, set for the time of year, so 40% in Florida and 40% in Seattle mean the same thing: four times the usual odds.
